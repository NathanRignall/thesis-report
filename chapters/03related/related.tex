\section{Phase Splitting}

Previous works from \textcite{zhongDistServeDisaggregatingPrefill2024} and \textcite{patelSplitwiseEfficientGenerative2024} introduced the concepts of \textit{phase splitting} LLM requests into prefill and decode.
Phase splitting is a technique that separates the two distinct computational stages of LLM inference, as described in \autoref{chap:prefill-decode}, onto separate hardware resources.
This tecnique targets improving the overall cost efficiency of LLM inference, by improving the utilisation of hardware.

Running both compute phases on the same physical hardware often leads to inconsistent end-to-end latencies  \autocite{patelSplitwiseEfficientGenerative2024}.
This is because there is typically strong prefill-decode interference as a result of batching, where each phase waits for the other to complete (to compete for GPU resources) \autocite{zhongDistServeDisaggregatingPrefill2024}.
To solve these latency inconsistencies, service providers have resulted to overrprovisioning resources to meet service level objectives (SLOs) \autocite{zhongDistServeDisaggregatingPrefill2024}.
However, this results in sub-optimal use of hardware resources where available compute is often underutilised.
This problem further worsens when the compute requirements for prefill and decode do not match each other, which is often the case in real-world scenarios.
% common workload scenarios \autoref{inference-challanges}

Also, executing both prefill and decode on the same hardware limits the possible deployment scenarios of models, as prefill and decode have to use the same parallelism configurations \autocite{zhongDistServeDisaggregatingPrefill2024}.
Different parallelism techniques may be more optimal for prefill as compared to decode and visa versa.
Hence, phase splitting provides important efficiency gains for LLM inference while demand from users is ever increasing.

\section{Heterogenous Serving}

Building upon the concepts introduced with \textit{phase splitting}, systems started to employ advanced scheduling methods to separate the prefill and decode phases onto specialised hardware.
As the prefill and decode phase have different compute requirements it makes sense to target the workload deployment onto the most optimal hardware for the task.
This is particularly relevant in heterogeneous environments such as the cloud, where a diverse mix of GPUs with varying costs, compute power, and memory bandwidths are available.

 The schedulers introduced with Splitwise and DistServe both operate over a very small search space (due to the homogenous hardware).
 Hence, \textcite{jiangThunderServeHighperformanceCostefficient2025} and \textcite{jiangHexGenGenerativeInference2024} work to develop more advanced scheduling algorithms, which optimise the time-to-solution and overall solution quality.
